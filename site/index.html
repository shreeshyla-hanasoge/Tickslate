What is Tickslate?

A dashboard is a page of small tiles, each showing one thing: a number and how it moved, a trend, a list. Tickslate gives you those tiles ready-made, and a grid to put them in that each user can arrange for themselves. You hand each tile your data; it does the drawing.

  • A grid people can rearrange

    Tiles come in seven sizes and reflow from four columns on a wide screen to two on a phone. People press and hold to move or resize a tile, remove the ones they don't need, and undo a mistake. Your app saves their arrangement with one line of code.

  • Tiles for any data

    A headline number with its change and trend; line, area and bar charts; a progress bar toward a goal or a limit; a sortable table; and a plain card for anything else, even a chart from another library.

  • Built for trading apps too

    Today's profit and loss, account growth over time with its deepest dip, market open and close clocks, a watchlist, open positions, a calendar of winning and losing days, trading statistics and account funding.

  • Works anywhere. Standard web components with nothing else to install. This site is built the same way: plain HTML and no framework.
  • Your brand. Every colour, corner and font is a setting (a --ts-* CSS variable). Six ready-made themes are included.
  • Accessible. Works by keyboard, says out loud what each tile shows, and never uses colour alone for gains and losses.

Install

With no build step

<script type="module" src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/tickslate@__TICKSLATE_VERSION__/elements.js"></script>

One tag loads every widget. The URL pins an exact version, so a new release never changes your page.

Need a classic script instead? cdn/tickslate.iife.js from the same address loads everything and sets window.Tickslate.

With a bundler

npm install tickslate
import 'tickslate/elements'

That registers <ts-grid> and every widget. Each widget also has its own entry, such as tickslate/elements/metric, so you pay only for what you use.

Built for trading apps

The finance tiles know what a trading screen needs: a sign and an arrow on every gain and loss, session times in the exchange's own zone, figures that update in place without moving the layout, and a header that says when the data has gone stale. These six tiles run on the same made-up numbers as the board above. This one is locked, as a finished app's would be.

Want this as a page? The playground runs a full trading board with a schedule that swaps layouts at the market close, and its Copy HTML button gives you the page.

Accessible by default

  • Keyboard editing. Tab to a tile, Space to pick it up, the arrow keys to move it, Space to drop, and plus or minus to resize.
  • Tiles that say what they show. A screen reader hears “Users, 18,420, up 4.1% vs Sunday”, not just “Users”, and each chart states its result in words.
  • Never colour alone. Gains carry a plus sign and losses a true minus, each with an arrow, so the meaning survives any palette.
  • Forced colours and reduced motion. Boundaries, focus rings and chart lines stay visible in high-contrast modes, and nothing wiggles when motion is turned down.

Your theme, a few variables

Tiles read their look from --ts-* CSS variables, so matching your brand is a handful of lines, never a fork. Paper is the light theme and Graphite the dark one; data-theme="auto" follows the system.

:root {
  --ts-accent: #0b6bcb;
  --ts-radius: 6px;
  --ts-font-ui: 'Inter', system-ui, sans-serif;
}

Six presets ship as stylesheets: Neutral, Paper, Graphite, Calm, Signal and Terminal. Try one on the board at the top, or read the theming guide.
